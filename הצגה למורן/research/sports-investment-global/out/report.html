1 The short answer

There is no single official "sport spending per capita" league table. The closest thing that is real, official and cross-country comparable is Eurostat's COFOG "recreational & sporting services" series — on it, general government spends about €151 per resident (0.4% of GDP) across the EU-27, ranging from ~€24 (Bulgaria) to €700–1,100 (Luxembourg, Iceland).

Two traps make raw "per capita / per athlete" numbers misleading, and both are handled below: (a) scope — a national sport agency budget is ~10× smaller than total government sport spending (most sport money is municipal); (b) denominator — "per athlete" means something completely different for a club member (€10–90) than for a state-funded elite athlete (€12k–100k).

2 The one comparable metric Leading evidence

Eurostat's gov_10a_exp dataset reports general government expenditure on "recreational and sporting services" (COFOG group GF08.1) for every EU/EFTA country on an identical definition — all levels of government (central + regional + municipal), same currency (EUR), same year. This is the only place the "per capita" question has a clean, official, comparable answer. Two official columns below (% of GDP, straight from Eurostat) plus a derived per-capita (Eurostat total ÷ population).

Germany's "€10 per member" and the UK's "£83k per athlete" are both correct and both official-derived — they answer different questions. A per-member figure divided into elite funding, or vice-versa, produces nonsense (we discarded e.g. a "S$4.8m per Singapore scholar" figure that wrongly divided the whole agency budget by 107 elite scholars).

5 Caveats before you quote a number Method

  1. Scope is everything. Eurostat COFOG = all levels of government (municipal dominates). National-agency budgets (§3) are a fraction of that. Never rank a COFOG per-capita against an agency per-capita.
  2. Recreation vs sport. COFOG GF08.1 bundles recreation (parks, playgrounds) with sport. It is the accepted proxy but slightly overstates "sport."
  3. Currency. §2 is natively in EUR (comparable). §3 non-EU per-capita carries a rough €-equivalent at ~2025 rates — indicative only, not an official conversion.
  4. Per-capita figures marked "~" are our arithmetic on official numerators (budget/total) and standard populations — the inputs are official, the division is ours.
  5. Athlete counts are not standardised. "Licence", "membership", "registered", "carded", "beneficiary" all differ by country — the denominator drives the per-athlete number more than the money does.
  6. Gulf states & some others publish no consolidated sport budget, so an official per-capita is genuinely unavailable (recorded as such, not estimated).
